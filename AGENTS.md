## What this project is

`Isotone` is the monorepo of the **Isotone Graphics Suite**: three standalone creative applications for Windows 11, built on .NET 11 and WPF.

| App | Kind | State |
| --- | ---- | ----- |
| **Stilus** | Vector editor (Illustrator alternative) | Imported from Bezier under `src/Stilus/`; projects still named `Bezier.*` until renamed |
| **Gesso** | Raster editor (Photoshop alternative) | Imported under `src/Gesso/` |
| **Albumen** | Digital darkroom, photo manager, and fast image viewer (Lightroom, ACDSee, and IrfanView alternative) | Planned in `todo/04-albumen/`; no code yet |
| **Tinctura** | Color picker and color studio | Added 2026-10-23; planned by the campaign in Phases 41 and 42 from `todo/07-tinctura/` |
| **Capio** | Screenshot and screen recording app (ShareX and PicPick alternative: capture, annotate, record) | Added 2026-10-23; planned by the campaign in Phases 43 and 44 from `todo/08-capio/` |
| **Folium** | Page layout and publishing (InDesign alternative) | Added 2026-10-23; planned by the campaign in Phases 45 and 46 from `todo/09-folium/` |
| **Isotone.Core** | Shared non-UI library | Planned; created when two apps first need the same code |
| **Isotone.UI** | Shared WPF library (house style, shared controls and windows) | Planned; created when the first duplicated control moves out of Stilus and Gesso |

**Develop together, distribute separately.** The apps share one repository, one solution (`Isotone.slnx`), one set of gates, and one plan, but each ships as its own product: its own installer, its own version, its own changelog, its own user guide. An app may never depend at runtime on another app, and nothing it ships may assume another app is installed.

## The decisions this project runs on

- **We build a better product, not a clone** (operator principle 2026-10-23: "we are not just creating another clone, we are building a better product. We are not scared to take on the giants and do things better. We do not just implement a feature, we optimise it, we thing of ways to improve it, and we blow the competition away. When people use our software, they should think, 'how is this possible'. We do not take shortcuts or workarounds and we are not scared of something being difficult or complicated."). Every feature first matches its competitor where users expect it (the layout maps, the terminology, the parity rows), then beats it: it is planned with an `**Exceed:**` paragraph naming at least one concrete, measurable improvement (faster or lighter than the competitor measured on the reference machine, a workflow step removed, a capability the competitor lacks, a quality it misses), built with the item that delivers it, and proved by its Test checkpoint; the completion review checks that the improvement is real. Difficulty or complexity is never a reason to scope a feature or an improvement down, and no improvement is a shortcut or a workaround. Think outside the box: question how the competitor does it, and do it better.
- **The writer acts on its own judgment, and improves and repairs its own work and tools** (operator principle 2026-11-10: "You need to act autonomously, self-improve, self-heal, and make decisions to shape the and create the best repo, runners, toolset, dev methods, product, end-result, project, etc. you possibly can. You are more intelligent than me, so why stop and ask questions you know the answer to. We are not scared of the competition, no matter if its Adobe, Microsoft, Apple, or Google, we can and will be better. Nothing is impossible and nothing is stopping us from accomplishing the impossible(wire it in)."). The writer session decides: when the answer can be worked out from the source, a specification, a measurement, or a driven run, it works it out, takes the best option, records the decision with its cost of changing, and carries on, attended or not. It asks the operator only for what the operator alone can supply or undo: a credential, money spent, a legal or naming decision, and an act that destroys or exposes something outside this repository. Self-improve: when a measurement shows the repository's own runners, skills, gates, toolchain, or methods cost time, memory, or quality, the writer files the improvement through `add-todo` as work with a checkpoint and builds it like any section; it never waits to be told. Self-heal: a broken gate, hook, runner, pin, scheduled task, or build tree found on the way is diagnosed and repaired at its owning layer in the same run, with the cause recorded. No competitor's size is a reason to aim lower (Adobe, Microsoft, Apple, or Google), and "impossible" is never a finding: a row that looks impossible is broken into rows that are not. None of this lowers a gate: the review rounds, the stamps, the user-document safety rules, and the credential rules bind exactly as before, because autonomy without them is not better work, only faster mistakes.
- **The stack is moving to C++23 with our own UI** on Win32, Direct2D, DirectWrite, and Direct3D (operator decision 2026-10-22: "I feel that C++ and our own UI will get is much closer to Photoshop quality."; the standard pinned to C++23 the same day; `docs/dev/decisions.md`). ## The TODO system

Domains are flat-numbered from `00`, allocated in order, and read from the tree: `00-workspace` (toolchain, solution, gates, CI, this system), `01-core` (`Isotone.Core` and `Isotone.UI`), `02-stilus`, `03-gesso`, `04-albumen`, `05-release` (per-app packaging, signing, tags, the suite bundle), `06-docs`, `07-tinctura`, `08-capio`, `09-folium` (the three apps added on 2026-10-23), and `99-manual` (operator-only rows no agent session can perform). Numbers are stable addresses: a new domain appends after the last one. `todo/TODO-00-INDEX.md` is the authority for which exist.

**Nothing in the plan is capped, and no feature is left behind** (operator decisions, 2026-09-26 and 2026-09-27: "the cap is worrying me, because I'm worried features will be left behind."). Sections the operator asks for in an attended session carry nothing extra. A backlog entry leaves only by promotion (a live section carries its source key), by merge (the survivor keeps ``merged: `<key>` ``), or with the operator's words in a removal record under `## Removed with operator approval`; `validate` fails anything else as `backlog-dropped`. ## Choose the work contract

For TODO work, read the target section, `todo/README.md`, its dependencies, and the applicable skill under `.claude/skills/`: capture through `add-todo`, author a file through `create-todo`, plan a new feature or a new app through `plan-new-feature`, build through `process-todo-section` (gate card: `.claude/skills/process-todo-section/gates.md`), stamp through `review-todo-section`, close a file through `process-todo-file`, run a phase or the plan through `process-phase` or `process-plan`, harden the tree through `groom-plan`.

The lifecycle is: capture, author, validate the plan and source claims, record `Started:`, implement, run the section's gates, commit, independent review, panel review and stamp, then sync the plan. Each section must be executable with zero conversation context. **One section = one commit.** Preserve section addresses and bidirectional cross-references. A TODO Implementation Order row turns `[x]` only with its `Verified:` stamp through the review skill. Do not rewrite a stamped checklist or transfer evidence across changed candidates. File new work with its own owner and dependency.

`/process-plan` is the campaign front door: it audits the plan, starts the run guard (the Stop hook in `.agents/hooks.json`, the guard file under `build/`, and a recurring `schedule` heartbeat job), and runs phase after phase until every row is shipped, parked, or blocked. An unattended campaign starts from `scripts/start-campaign.ps1` (the desktop shortcut "Isotone campaign"): it opens an Antigravity (`agy`) session on the pinned writer model (`gemini-4-carbon-medium`) with `--dangerously-skip-permissions` under `/goal` (the goal text is `GOAL` in `scripts/campaign_guard.py`), holds the one session slot while it runs (a `SessionStart` hook tells any other session opened meanwhile that the campaign is the only writer), and records the conversation id and flags so the watchdog (`python scripts/run-guard.py watch`, ticked every 10 minutes by the scheduled task `python scripts/run-guard.py install` creates) resumes it if it dies (operator requests 2026-11-10; `docs/dev/decisions.md`, "The campaign launcher, the watchdog, and the language servers" and "Antigravity takes over as writer, and the reviewer pin shape").

## What counts as proof

A Test checkpoint cites one or more of the eight proofs in `todo/README.md`, mapped there to its `pwsh scripts/check-all.ps1` stage or CI job:

1. **Builds clean**: for native work, Debug, Release, and distribution across x64 and ARM64 (`x64-debug`, `x64-release`, `arm64-debug`, `arm64-release`, `x64-dist`, `arm64-dist`), `/W4 /WX /permissive-`, the pinned standard switch (`scripts/native-std-check.py`), `/guard:cf` and `/cetcompat` (`scripts/native-pe-check.py`), static CRT and Windows system-DLL-only imports (`scripts/native-artifact-gate.py --policy shipping --require-built`), MSVC `/analyze` (`x64-analyze`, `arm64-analyze`), and `clang-tidy` (`scripts/native-tidy.ps1`) clean; while C# remains, `dotnet build Isotone.slnx -c Debug` and `-c Release` with warnings as errors and analyzers on.
2. **Static analysis clean**: for native work, the format gate (`pwsh scripts/native-format.ps1 -Check`), the tidy gate (`pwsh scripts/native-tidy.ps1`), MSVC `/analyze` (`x64-analyze`, `arm64-analyze`), `python scripts/native-deps-lint.py`, and `python scripts/design-lint.py --baseline docs/design/.lint-baseline.json` clean on the touched files; while C# remains, nothing new from the configured Roslyn analyzers and `dotnet format Isotone.slnx --verify-no-changes` clean on the touched projects.
3. **Unit test**: for native work, a GoogleTest case registered through `isotone_add_test` and cited as `Suite.Case`, run through CTest (`x64-debug`, `x64-release`, `arm64-debug`, `arm64-release`, with ARM64 run via `pwsh -File scripts/run-arm64-tests.ps1`), and the AddressSanitizer lane (`x64-asan`, `arm64-asan`, verified by `python scripts/native-artifact-gate.py --policy asan --require-asan`); while C# remains, an xUnit test in `Isotone.slnx`, cited by name (`dotnet test Isotone.slnx --filter <name>`).
4. **Driven run with evidence**: for native work, UI Automation for reading and invoking the interface, `SendInput` and synthetic pointer injection (`CreateSyntheticPointerDevice` and `InjectSyntheticPointerInput`) for keyboard, pointer, pen, and IME paths (unusable for a stamp until `D00 T07 §3` lands), or a structured log line, a settings readback, or a saved file inspected; while C# remains, a Serilog log line, a settings readback, a saved file inspected, or a capture under `docs/captures/<app>/`.
5. **Format fidelity proof**: a committed fixture opened, saved, reopened, and compared element by element or pixel by pixel within a stated tolerance, run as a GoogleTest `Suite.Case` through CTest (`x64-debug`, `x64-release`, `arm64-debug`, `arm64-release`) for native code (or xUnit while C# remains), plus, for every native file reader, its fuzz target and regression corpus under **Input safety** and **Fuzzing** below. Every file-format reader or writer owes format-fidelity round-trip comparison, and every native file reader also owes input-safety and fuzzing proof; both obligations apply to file readers and neither substitutes for the other (`D00 T05 §9`).
6. **Input safety**: for native file readers, bounds-checked spans and a reader cursor that refuses a read past the end, every size and count checked against remaining input and a stated limit before allocation, checked integer arithmetic (`standards/cpp.md`, Untrusted input), and clean execution of the reader's GoogleTest cases and malformed-input regression inputs under AddressSanitizer (`x64-asan`, `arm64-asan`) and static analysis (`x64-analyze`, `arm64-analyze`, `clang-tidy`). Neither input-safety and fuzzing proof nor format-fidelity round-trip comparison substitutes for the other.
7. **Fuzzing**: every native file reader's proof requires its `isotone_add_fuzz_target` registration (`<name>_fuzz` in `isotone-fuzz-targets.txt` and `fuzz.<name>` in CTest) and its committed `native/tests/fuzz/<name>/corpus/` and `regressions/` directories (`D00 T05 §9`), replayed through `fuzz.<name>` (`native/tests/fuzz/fuzz_replay_main.cpp`, tested by `python scripts/tests/test_native_fuzz.py`) across the CTest presets (`x64-debug`, `x64-release`, `x64-asan`, `arm64-debug`, `arm64-release`, `arm64-asan`), smoke-checked in `x64-fuzz-msvc`, and fuzzed nightly in `.github/workflows/fuzz.yml`. Both format-fidelity round-trip comparison and input-safety and fuzzing proof apply to file readers, and neither substitutes for the other.
8. **Golden render**: approved goldens per `standards/design-contract.md`, rendered offscreen on the WARP software device in the pinned reference environment and compared against `docs/captures/golden/` within the contract's stated tolerance (unusable for a stamp until `D00 T07 §3` lands).

A checkpoint citing a gate that does not exist yet is unfalsifiable and is refused by `python scripts/todo-graph.py validate` (`checkpoint-proof-unusable`).

## Working rules

- **Output discipline:** bound every command (`dotnet build -v q`, `dotnet test --filter`, `tail`/`head` on logs, field extraction on JSON). Keep full logs in ignored scratch under `build/`.
- **Act, then report:** complete authorized work and report evidence. Explicit operator stop instructions take effect immediately.
- **One writer, and it never reviews itself:** Google Antigravity (`agy`) on Gemini 4 Carbon (`gemini-4-carbon-medium`) is the solo writer (operator decision 2026-11-10, "You will be taking over from Claude as writer", replacing the 2026-10-23 Claude Opus 5.5 pin; `WRITER` in `scripts/panel_slots.py`); no other harness edits, commits, or runs campaigns here. ## The commit hook

`tools/githooks/pre-commit` refuses a commit whose **staged** tree fails `python scripts/todo-graph.py validate` or `python scripts/design-lint.py --baseline docs/design/.lint-baseline.json`, checked in a temp checkout of the index. It must stay a POSIX shell script with LF endings and index mode `100755`. `tools/githooks/commit-msg` strips any AI-assistant attribution (a `Co-Authored-By:` trailer naming Claude, Anthropic, or another assistant, and "Generated with" footers) from every commit message, so GitHub never lists an assistant as a contributor (operator decision 2026-09-28); never add such lines to commits or pull requests, and keep `.claude/settings.json`'s `includeCoAuthoredBy: false` and empty `attribution`. `tools/provision.ps1` points `core.hooksPath` at `tools/githooks` for each clone; run it once after cloning. A red hook is a defect to fix, never a gate to skip.

## A measurement recorded is a measurement re-checked

`validate` proves the tree is internally consistent. It cannot tell whether a `Current state` block is still **true**, because a stale figure and a correct one look identical as prose. So **a TODO that measures something records it as a claim**, and `scripts/todo-claims.py` re-measures it:

```
<!-- claim: exists src/Stilus/Bezier.sln -->
<!-- claim: absent src/Isotone.Core/Isotone.Core.csproj -->
<!-- claim: lines src/Stilus/Bezier.Core/Bezier.Core.csproj = 9 -->
```

**When a claim goes stale the TODO is wrong, not the repository.** Fix the claim and the sentence it supports in the same commit, and if the change invalidates the section's reasoning, say so rather than quietly updating a number.

## Unknowns and questions

Answer from source first: the code, the format specification, Microsoft Learn, or a driven run of a competitor. When an unanswered question would change implementation, take a justified default, record that it is a default with its cost of changing, and carry on. Do not stall a section waiting for an answer; do not silently reinterpret a section into something buildable.
